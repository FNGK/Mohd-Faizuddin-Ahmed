# How to Get Cited by AI: ChatGPT & Perplexity

**Quick answer:** To get cited by AI engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity, publish content with a trait they reward: **original data**, **clear attributable claims**, **entity trust**, and **extractable passages**, then make sure the bots can actually read the page. Citation goes to sources that are specific, clear, trusted, and easy to lift. This guide is for SEO managers, content leads, and founders.

_Last reviewed: 7 September 2026._

In classic search, the prize was position one. In AI search, the prize is being the source the answer quotes and names. It's a different game with a surprisingly learnable rulebook, because when you look at what actually gets cited, the same handful of traits show up again and again.

## How AI engines pick their sources

Answer engines don't cite from memory; they retrieve. When someone asks a question, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) pull candidate passages from live sources, then generate an answer that credits a few of them. That means two things must be true for you to be cited: your page has to be **retrievable** (crawlable and indexed), and one of its passages has to be the **best available answer** to the specific question. Get either wrong and you're invisible, no matter how good the rest of the site is.

## What actually gets cited

Reverse-engineer enough citations and four traits keep appearing. Aim for all four on your most important pages.

### Original data or examples
Original data is the strongest magnet for a citation. A statistic, a benchmark, a first-hand example, or a result only you can report gives a model something specific to attribute to you by name. Restating what everyone already says makes you interchangeable; a number or example that exists nowhere else makes you the obvious source to cite. Publish what you uniquely know.

### Clear, attributable claims
Clear claims get cited because a model can lift them cleanly. A hedged, rambling paragraph gives an engine nothing quotable, while a direct, self-contained statement is easy to attribute and drop into an answer. Say the thing plainly, in a sentence that stands on its own, and you make yourself easy to quote, which is exactly what earns the citation.

### Trust in your entity
Entity trust decides whether a model believes you enough to cite you. Engines reason about who you are and what you're known for, built from consistent details and credible mentions across the web, not just links. If recognised sources reference you and your information is consistent everywhere, you become a source worth trusting, and trust is what converts a retrieved passage into a named citation.

### An extractable passage
An extractable passage is the physical unit an engine lifts. Even great information fails if it's buried in a wall of text, because the model can't cleanly separate the answer from the noise. Lead each section with a direct answer, keep it self-contained, and use structure, headings, lists, and tables, so a clean chunk is sitting right there, ready to be quoted and credited.

## Let the bots read you

None of the above matters if an engine can't crawl the page. Confirm you aren't blocking AI crawlers such as `GPTBot` or `PerplexityBot` in robots.txt, ship fast server-rendered HTML, and add complete schema (structured data) so machines parse your meaning. Google's [crawler overview](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/overview-google-crawlers) lists the bots, and its [structured data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) covers the markup that corroborates your content. This is table stakes; without access, the best content on the web still won't be cited.

## Search experience (SXO)

Getting cited brings the visitor. The page still has to convert them. Run each key page through a quick search-experience check:

- Does the page lead with the direct answer in the first screen?
- Is the primary action (call, form, or booking) visible without scrolling?
- Does the page carry complete schema (structured data) for its type?
- Is there a unique data point or example a model would want to quote?
- Would a first-time visitor know their next step in five seconds?

Ask that last one honestly. A citation that lands on a page nobody can act on is a wasted win.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do AI engines choose which sources to cite?
AI engines choose sources by retrieving candidate passages that match the question, then citing the ones that best answer it from trusted, accessible pages. Retrieval comes first, so a page has to be crawlable and indexed, and selection favours clear, self-contained passages backed by a recognised entity. In short, engines cite the source that most cleanly and credibly answers the exact question asked.

### Does ChatGPT cite websites?
Yes, ChatGPT cites websites when it uses its search capability to answer a question, naming and linking the sources it drew from. Those citations go to pages it can retrieve and that clearly answer the query. If your page is blocked from crawlers, buried in heavy scripts, or vague on the topic, ChatGPT has nothing clean to cite, so accessibility and clarity decide whether you appear.

### Do backlinks help you get cited by AI?
Backlinks help indirectly. They still feed the authority and trust signals that make an engine more willing to cite you, but they're no longer the whole story. Consistent entity information and credible brand mentions, even unlinked ones, also shape whether a model trusts your source. Treat backlinks as one input into entity trust, not as the single lever that earns an AI citation.

### How long does it take to get cited by AI?
How long it takes to get cited by AI varies. Fixing access and restructuring a page for extractable answers can influence citations within weeks, because retrieval updates as engines recrawl. Building the entity trust that makes you a default source compounds over months, much like traditional authority. Expect quick wins from structure and access, and a slower, steadier climb from the reputation signals that make you consistently cited.

## The takeaway

Getting cited by ChatGPT and Perplexity isn't luck. Give engines something original to quote, say it clearly, earn the trust that makes you believable, structure the page so a clean passage is easy to lift, and make sure the bots can read you. Do that on your priority pages and you stop competing for a link and start becoming the answer.
