AI Search Readiness Audit: A Free Template

AI Search Readiness Audit: A Free Template

Quick answer: An AI search readiness audit scores your site across four sections, entities and authority, content and extractability, technical access, and measurement, then tells you which gap to fix first. Score each out of 100, average them, and start with the lowest. This guide is for SEO managers, marketing leads, and founders who want a plan, not more theory.

Last reviewed: 7 September 2026.

Most AI search advice tells you what matters and leaves you to guess where you actually stand. This audit fixes that. Run it in an afternoon, get an honest score, and walk away with a prioritised list instead of a vague sense that you "should do more with AI."

How to score it

Rate each of the four sections below out of 100 based on the checklist items it contains, roughly, the share of items you can honestly tick. Average the four scores for an overall readiness number. Don't inflate it; the point is to find the weakest link, not to feel good. A site scoring 90 on content and 20 on measurement isn't "average at 55", it's flying blind, and the low score is where the next win lives. These four sections map to the signals engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI features use to find and cite sources.

The four sections to audit

Entities and authority

Entities and authority measure whether engines trust who you are. Score yourself on these:

  • You have clear entity pages (organisation, people) with Organization and sameAs schema.
  • Your name, address, and details are consistent everywhere online.
  • Credible sites mention your brand, linked or not.
  • Named authors with real credentials appear on key pages.

Content and extractability

Content and extractability measure whether your pages can be lifted into an answer. Score yourself on these:

  • Key sections lead with a direct, self-contained answer.
  • Headings are phrased as real questions people ask.
  • You use tables, lists, and definitions where the content allows.
  • Each important topic has its own page, not one page for everything.

Technical access

Technical access measures whether the bots can read you at all. Score yourself on these:

  • You are not blocking GPTBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended in robots.txt (check Google's crawler overview).
  • Pages load fast and render their content in server-side HTML.
  • Complete schema (structured data) is in place for each page type.
  • No key content is trapped behind heavy client-side scripts.

Measurement

Measurement is the section most sites fail, and the fastest to fix. Score yourself on these:

  • A GA4 custom channel group separates AI referral traffic.
  • You track citation share of voice across a fixed set of prompts.
  • AI visibility is reported separately, not folded into organic.
  • You review the trend at least monthly.

Turn the score into a plan

Now use the numbers. List the four scores, and start with the lowest, not the one you enjoy most. For most teams that's measurement, because you can't improve what you can't see, followed by technical access, since fixing crawlability is fast and unblocks everything else. Content and entity work compound over time, so schedule them as ongoing, not one-off. If you want a deeper, page-level version of this, our SEO audit playbook extends the same four-section logic.

Re-run it quarterly

An audit is a snapshot, not a certificate. AI engines change, competitors improve, and pages drift, so re-run this quarterly and track the trend of your overall score. A rising number is proof the work is landing, exactly the kind of evidence that keeps a stakeholder funding it. Our case studies show what that trend looks like once the fundamentals are in place.

Search experience (SXO)

Readiness is only half the job; the pages still have to convert. As you audit, check each key page for search experience too:

  • Does the page lead with the answer in the first screen?
  • Is the primary action (call, form, or booking) obvious?
  • Does the page carry complete schema (structured data)?
  • Is there one clear next step, not five competing ones?
  • Would a first-time visitor know what to do in five seconds?

If a page scores well on readiness but a visitor can't act on it, you've optimised for traffic you can't convert.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI search readiness audit?

An AI search readiness audit is a structured check of how prepared your site is to be found and cited by AI answer engines. It scores four sections, entities and authority, content and extractability, technical access, and measurement, and produces an overall number plus a prioritised fix list. Unlike a generic SEO audit, it focuses specifically on the signals ChatGPT, Perplexity, and AI Overviews use to select and cite sources.

How do you audit a site for AI search?

You audit a site for AI search by scoring it against the four sections in this template. Check whether engines can crawl and trust you, whether your content is structured to be lifted, whether your entity signals are consistent, and whether you measure citations at all. Tick the items you honestly meet in each section, average the scores, and then act on the lowest-scoring section first rather than spreading effort thinly.

Which AI readiness section matters most?

No single section matters most in isolation, because your weakest one caps the rest. That said, measurement is where most sites score lowest and should usually be fixed first, since you can't improve AI visibility you can't see. Technical access is the close second, because a page the bots can't read scores zero everywhere else regardless of how good the content and entity signals are.

How often should I re-run the audit?

Re-run the audit quarterly for most teams, or monthly if AI search is a strategic priority. Quarterly is frequent enough to catch drift as engines and competitors change, without turning the audit into busywork. The value comes from tracking the trend of your overall score over time, so keep the scoring consistent between runs and note what you changed, so improvements are easy to attribute.

The takeaway

Stop guessing where you stand in AI search. Score the four sections honestly, average them, and fix the lowest first, usually measurement, then access. Re-run it quarterly and watch the number climb. That's the whole method: simple, repeatable, and far more useful than another article telling you AI is important.
