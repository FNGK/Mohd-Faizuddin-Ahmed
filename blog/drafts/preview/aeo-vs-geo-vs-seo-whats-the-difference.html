AEO vs GEO vs SEO: What's the Difference?

FA Mohd Faizuddin Ahmed September 7, 2026 5 min read

Quick answer: SEO, AEO, GEO. Three acronyms, endless confusion, and a lot of vendors pretending they're at war.

AEO vs GEO vs SEO: What's the Difference?

Quick answer: AEO vs GEO vs SEO is not a rivalry, it's a stack. SEO earns the crawl, the index, and the authority. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) makes you the direct answer to a question. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) gets you cited inside AI answers on ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. Same foundation, three targets. This guide is for marketing leads, founders, and SEO managers.

Last reviewed: 7 September 2026.

Every few months a new acronym arrives and someone declares the old one dead. SEO is dead, long live AEO. AEO is dead, long live GEO. It's mostly noise. The three terms describe layers of the same work, and understanding how they fit together saves you from buying three "strategies" when you need one.

What each term actually means

Strip away the marketing and the definitions are simple.

What is SEO?

Search Engine Optimization is the foundation: helping search engines crawl, understand, and rank your pages. It covers technical health, content relevance, authority, and structured data. Google's own SEO starter guide still describes this layer well, and nothing above it works without it.

What is AEO?

Answer Engine Optimization is about being the direct answer to a question, in a featured snippet, a voice result, or a "People also ask" box. It rewards content that answers a specific question cleanly and concisely, in a passage an engine can lift whole. Think of AEO as optimising for the one-box answer at the top, not the ten blue links beneath it.

What is GEO?

Generative Engine Optimization is about being pulled into generated answers across AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini, and inside Google's own AI features in Search. These systems retrieve passages, then write an answer that cites a few sources. GEO is the work of becoming one of those cited sources. Where AEO targets a single answer box, GEO targets the AI's synthesised response and the citation that rides along with it.

How they overlap and differ

Here's the whole thing in one view.

SEO AEO GEO
Goal Rank a page Be the direct answer Be cited in AI answers
Where it shows Classic results Snippets, voice, PAA ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini
Wins on Authority + relevance Clear, concise answers Trust + extractable passages
Core metric Rankings, clicks Answer capture rate Citation share of voice

Notice how much the "wins on" row shares. Clean structure and clear answers help all three. That's why treating them as separate budgets is a mistake, the same page, structured well, can rank in classic search, win a snippet, and get cited by an AI engine at once.

Which metrics belong to which

The confusion usually starts with measurement, because the reporting looks different even when the work overlaps.

  • SEO is measured on rankings, organic clicks, and impressions.
  • AEO is measured on how often you capture the snippet or answer box for target questions.
  • GEO is measured on citation share of voice, how often AI engines name and cite you versus competitors.

If your reports only show the SEO line, you're blind to two-thirds of the picture in 2026. A quick way to find the gaps across all three is our SEO audit playbook, which looks at the same page from each angle.

Which one should you focus on first

For almost every team, the honest answer is: get SEO right, then layer AEO and GEO on top. Weak fundamentals cap everything above them, an engine can't cite a page it can't crawl, and it won't feature a page that has no clear answer on it.

The good news is that the AEO and GEO layers don't need a separate content operation. They need your existing pages restructured, answer-first passages, question-led headings, complete structured data, so the same content works harder. That restructuring is the core of our content SEO work, and you can see the results in our case studies.

Search experience (SXO)

None of this matters if the visitor who finds you doesn't convert. Before you obsess over acronyms, run each key page through a quick search-experience check:

  • Does the page answer the exact question that brought the visitor, in the first screen?
  • Is the primary action (call, form, or booking) visible without scrolling?
  • Does the page carry complete schema (structured data) for its type?
  • Are the headings phrased as real questions a buyer would ask?
  • Would a first-time visitor know their next step in five seconds?

Ask yourself that last one honestly. Winning the snippet and losing the visitor is still losing.

Frequently asked questions

Is AEO the same as GEO?

AEO and GEO are close cousins, not the same thing. AEO focuses on being the single, direct answer to a question, in a featured snippet or voice result. GEO focuses on being cited inside a longer, AI-generated answer that stitches several sources together. They share almost all their tactics, so in practice you optimise for both at once, but GEO is the broader, newer term aimed specifically at generative engines.

Does GEO replace SEO?

GEO does not replace SEO; it depends on it. Generative engines still crawl, index, and weigh authority using the same machinery SEO has always addressed, then add a citation-selection step on top. If your SEO foundation is weak, GEO has nothing to build on, because a page that can't be crawled or trusted won't be pulled into an AI answer in the first place. Think layer, not replacement.

Which should a local business focus on first?

A local business should get SEO and Google Business Profile fundamentals right before chasing GEO. For a clinic or a local service, most demand still runs through local search and the map pack, so a strong, consistent local presence is the priority. Once that foundation is solid, the same well-structured pages naturally start earning AEO snippets and GEO citations without a separate project.

Do AEO and GEO need different content?

AEO and GEO don't need different content so much as better-structured content. The same page can serve both if it leads with a direct answer, uses question-led headings, and carries clean structured data. You're not writing two versions; you're making one version extractable enough that an answer box and an AI engine can both lift a passage from it and cite you.

The takeaway

Stop shopping for three separate strategies. AEO and GEO are not competitors to SEO; they're what you unlock when your SEO foundation is strong and your pages are structured to be lifted. Fix the fundamentals, make every page answer-first and machine-readable, and the same work pays off across classic search, answer boxes, and AI engines.
